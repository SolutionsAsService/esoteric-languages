Esoteric language

Chef

A program is a recipe: ingredients hold the numbers and mixing bowls are the stacks.

By David Morgan-Mar, 2002.

Ingredients. 72 g haricot beans 101 eggs 108 g lard 111 ml oil 32 zests 119 ml water 114 g red salmon 100 g dijon mustard 33 potatoes

What it is

A Chef program is a recipe. The ingredients are variables, each with an amount, and the method is the program. Mixing bowls and baking dishes are stacks: you put ingredients into a bowl, pour the bowl into a dish, and what is in the dish when the recipe ends is what the program prints. The specification also asks that a program be a good recipe.

Whether a number comes out as a number or a letter depends on the measure. Dry ingredients (grams, pinches) print as numbers; liquid ones (millilitres, dashes) print as characters, and Liquefy turns dry into liquid. That is how a recipe full of eggs and lard says “Hello”.

This site runs a part of Chef: the instructions in the table. Auxiliary recipes and the random Mix are not supported.

The instructions used here

SentenceMeaning
Put x into the mixing bowl.Push the value of ingredient x onto the bowl
Fold x into the mixing bowl.Pop the top of the bowl into ingredient x
Add x. Remove x.Add to, or subtract from, the top of the bowl the value of x
Combine x. Divide x.Multiply, or integer-divide, the top of the bowl by x
Liquefy contents of the mixing bowl.Make everything in the bowl liquid, so it prints as characters
Pour contents of the mixing bowl into the baking dish.Copy the bowl to the dish, keeping the order
Verb the x. … Verb the x until verbed.A loop: it runs while x is not zero, and the closing line takes 1 off x
Serves 1.Print the first baking dish, from the top down

Numbered bowls and dishes (the 2nd mixing bowl), Take, Stir, Clean and Set aside also work; the programs here do not need them.

Hello, World!

749 characters; it prints Hello world! in 14 steps, one for each method sentence. The words are the usual Chef example, so the output is Hello world! with a small w and no newline.

The recipe pushes the letters last-to-first, because a stack gives back the last thing put in. Liquefy then turns the whole bowl into characters, and the dish is printed from the top: H, then e, and so on. The ingredient called zests has no measure, and is the space.

How it runs

A loop, to count down from C to A: the flour is the counter, starting at 3, and each time round puts the current letter in the bowl and takes 1 off it. It prints ABC in 20 steps.

The letters go in as C, B, A, so A is on top and prints first. The letters are liquid (ml), so they print as characters; the flour and sugar are dry and never reach the dish.
