# R3 Critic: Provocateur (Shklovsky)

**Anchor used:** Shklovsky, "Art as Technique": defamiliarization versus automatization; art makes the stone stony, lengthens perception.

**Observed in the work:**
- Resolved in substance. Poster Ook! tile: each Brainfuck symbol (+ [ > < -) now sits over its Ook pair (markup: `<span class="op"><b>+</b>Ook.<br>Ook.</span>`, 106 pairs in index.html). The viewer sees the orangutan words as the same machine re-spelled; the repetition of "Ook." under "+" is the device working, not wallpaper.
- ook.html poster repeats it at larger count; the 1059-character listing below it is the raw, undecoded strip, a deliberate contrast. Good.
- Wallpaper risk: none of the other devices (Whitespace grid, Piet bars, Befunge trail) is a uniform repeat; each is computed.
- New defect: in the 1280x800 first screen the Ook! tile's third row is cut at the tile bottom; the pairs under "[ > + + +" show half-height, "Ook! Ook?" sliced mid-glyph. The evidence claim "21 pairs visible whole" is false for the poster as seen; the cut line reads as accident, not device. Type is 8 px under 15 px symbols.

**Verdict:** OBJECT (concrete: clipped third row on the poster).

**Requested change:** Show only complete rows in the Ook! tile (cap height to two rows, or overflow hidden on whole-row boundary), so no pair is sliced.

**Statement of meaning:** One program, spelled twice: the symbol you know, the words an orangutan would say.

**Critic question:** If the viewer must squint at 8 px "Ook." to feel the translation, has perception been lengthened or merely strained?
