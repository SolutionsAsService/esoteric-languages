# Critic: Wit (Fletcher), R2

Anchor used: Alan Fletcher, The Art of Looking Sideways: wit as the shortest route between two ideas; the visual pun.

Observed in the work:
- The 906/906 gag is now found: Brainfuck and Ook! sit side by side, and "the Brainfuck program, in orangutan" is the whole joke in six words. The Discworld explanation is one clause ("The words are the Librarian's, from Terry Pratchett's Discworld"). Joke got, not explained.
- LOLCODE is set as a lolcat caption (white impact-style HAI 1.2 / VISIBLE "HELLO, WORLD!" / KTHXBYE on magenta). The form is the language's own joke; the design adds a pun, not a label.
- Malbolge before and after, "58 of its 64 characters rewritten as it ran", is a true visual pun: the program defaces itself in front of you. Brainfuck above the tape it leaves (H d W ! cells) shows cause and effect in one glance.
- Weaker: on the first screen the Ook! field's caption sits above a faded, low-contrast block of Ook. text, so the pun is read before the picture; the Piet field and the poster title carry no pun at all, which is acceptable.
- The Malbolge page repeats the before/after as a boxed, truncated line plus a 40-row table: the gag gets explained at length lower down, but it is below the joke, not in place of it.

Verdict: PASS

Requested change (one): On the Malbolge page, let the before/after pair show the whole 64 characters without clipping (the right edge is cut in the 1280 shot), so the defacing reads as complete.

Statement of meaning: Eight languages answer the same sentence, and the design lets each one's own gag, orangutans, lolcats, self-vandalism, do the talking.

Critic question: Does Piet, the one field with no joke of its own, need one, or is its silence the contrast that makes the others land?
