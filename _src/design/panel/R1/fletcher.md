Critic: Wit (Fletcher), R1

Anchor used: Alan Fletcher, "The Art of Looking Sideways": wit as the shortest route between two ideas; seeing one thing as another (visual pun).

Observed in the work:
- The home poster is the one joke the site makes by showing, not telling. Each field is the language's own program as its picture, so "Hello World!" is seen as a bar chart (Befunge's reversed string), a column of Ook!s, a bitmap of spaces and tabs (Whitespace) and a coloured skyline (Piet). Piet's poster field is a program that is a painting, and the whole grid is a Mondrian. That is a true pun: Piet the language and Piet Mondrian, one grid seen as two things.
- Whitespace's band draws the invisible visible (faint, black and red bars), which is the pun for that language. Ook!'s band is a wall of Ook. that looks like an orangutan's monologue. Both work.
- Scratch is a clean sideways turn: the control that is not a joke, in blue, the one field whose count is blocks, not steps.
- Against that, the copy explains. Ook! page: "The joke is a reference to the orangutan Librarian in Terry Pratchett's Discworld novels." Scratch: "proof that looking unusual is not what makes a language esoteric." The home line "Each field is a real program... The big number is how many steps" is a user manual for a poster that was already legible.
- The "906 / 906" for Brainfuck and Ook! is an accidental gag (same machine, a different dress) that the home screen never lets the reader find; the Ook! page then states it flatly.
- Inner pages are a uniform template of tables; only the bands carry the wit.

Verdict: PASS

Requested change (one): Cut the sentence on the Ook! page that explains the Discworld joke to one clause, and let the identical 906 / 906 carry the Ook! gag, by adding a single caption on the Ook! field of the poster: "same 906 steps, now in orangutan".

Statement of meaning: One grid seen as a painting, a chart, a wall of apes and a blank page: the site shows each language as the thing it pretends to be.

Critic question: Where on the first screen does a visitor get to find the joke without being told that the fields are programs?
