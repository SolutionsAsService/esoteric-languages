**Critic: Formalist (Arnheim), R1**

**Anchor:** Arnheim, visual weight and balance about the structural skeleton (centre, vertical/horizontal axes); the dominant field and the "center of gravity" of a composition.

**Observed:**
- The skeleton holds. Black bars and the 3-3-... grid read as one Mondrian frame; the 906/906 pair, 98 and 27 sit as a top row and 26/40/1/2 as a bottom row, so there are two horizontal tiers under a heavy header bar.
- Weight is carried by hue and size, not texture. Red Brainfuck (top left) and black Piet (bottom left, widest field, roughly 520 px) are the heaviest masses; they sit on the left and diagonal-balance the white Whitespace field (top right, light but largest area) and the blue Scratch (bottom right). That is a real asymmetric balance, as Mondrian intended.
- The eye lands first on the headline, then drops to the two giant "906" numerals in red and yellow. The numerals are the strongest figures, which is right: the step count is the stated subject. But 906 appears twice, side by side, and the repetition makes the top-left a heavy pair while the bottom row's "1" and "2" are tiny by comparison.
- The glyph textures (Ook! grey-on-yellow, Befunge's lower strip, Whitespace's dot grid) are dense but small and kept low in each field. They stay subordinate to the numeral and name, so they do not fight; they act as ground. Ook!'s pale text is nearly invisible, which keeps it quiet rather than noisy.
- Inner page (Piet): the band repeats the same black field, the same large type and the same image, with the black bar rule above each section. The force carries, but the band is a smaller, flatter echo: a single dominant mass at top, then a long, evenly weighted text column below. That is a legitimate hierarchy, not a collapse.

**Verdict:** PASS
