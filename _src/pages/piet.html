What it is

A Piet program is a picture made of codels, square cells in one of 18 colours (six hues in three lightnesses) plus white and black. Touching codels of one colour form a block. The interpreter moves from block to block, and what it does depends on how far the hue and the lightness change on the way.

Numbers live on a stack: a pile where the last number put on is the first taken off. Most commands take numbers off the stack or put them on.

The language is named after the painter Piet Mondrian. This site's grid of black bars and coloured fields borrows from him too, and its colours are Piet's own.

Colour change is the command

{{piettable}}

Push pushes the number of codels in the block being left; that is how numbers get into a Piet program. Black codels and the edges of the image are walls; white ones are crossed without a command.
