What it is

A Chef program is a recipe. The ingredients are variables, each with an amount, and the method is the program. Mixing bowls and baking dishes are stacks: you put ingredients into a bowl, pour the bowl into a dish, and what is in the dish when the recipe ends is what the program prints. The specification also asks that a program be a good recipe.

Whether a number comes out as a number or a letter depends on the measure. Dry ingredients (grams, pinches) print as numbers; liquid ones (millilitres, dashes) print as characters, and Liquefy turns dry into liquid. That is how a recipe full of eggs and lard says “Hello”.

This site runs a part of Chef: the instructions in the table. Auxiliary recipes and the random Mix are not supported.

Numbered bowls and dishes (the 2nd mixing bowl), Take, Stir, Clean and Set aside also work; the programs here do not need them.

How it runs

A loop, to count down from C to A: the flour is the counter, starting at 3, and each time round puts the current letter in the bowl and takes 1 off it. It prints {{output2}} in {{steps2}} steps.
